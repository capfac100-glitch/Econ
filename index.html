ONE-PAGE NOTE 1: Poverty Fundamentals & Measurement Frameworks

1. Multi-Dimensional Definition of Poverty

  • Overarching Concept: Poverty is defined as the deprivation of multiple dimensions of well-being, including health, education, income, clean environment, social relations, security, and political voice[cite: 1, 3].
  • World Bank Definition: Pronounced deprivation in well-being, comprising inability to acquire basic goods/services, low health/education, poor sanitation, inadequate physical security, lack of voice, and insufficient opportunity[cite: 1, 3].

2. Selection Criteria for Measurement Standards

Dimension Choice (Subjective) ──> Criteria Standard (Subjective) ──> Quantifiable Application (Objective)[cite: 1, 3]
  • Consumption Expenditure: Chosen metric because income and wealth are volatile and suffer from severe under-reporting[cite: 1, 3].

3. Poverty Line Basket (PLB) & Measures

  • Headcount Ratio (HCR): Counts how many are poor[cite: 1, 3].
  • Poverty Gap Index: Measures how poor the poor are (depth)[cite: 1, 3].
  • Squared Poverty Gap Index: Squares individual gaps to measure distribution severity among the poorest[cite: 1, 3]. $$\text{Squared Poverty Gap} = \frac{\sum (GAP_i)^2}{N} \text{[cite: 1, 3]}$$

ONE-PAGE NOTE 4: International Poverty Lines, SDGs & MPI

1. World Bank International Poverty Lines

  • Low-Income (Extreme Poverty): $2.15 (2017 PPP) updated to $3.00 (2021 PPP)[cite: 1, 3].
  • Lower-Middle Income (LMIC): $3.65 (2017 PPP) updated to $4.20 (2021 PPP)[cite: 1, 3].

2. Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)

  • Global MPI (OPHI/UNDP): 3 Dimensions (Health 1/3, Education 1/3, Standard of Living 1/3) & 10 Indicators[cite: 1, 3]. Deprivation cutoff $\ge 33.33\%$[cite: 1, 3].
  • National MPI (NITI Aayog): Adds 2 indicators: Antenatal Care and Bank Account (Total 12 indicators)[cite: 1, 3]. $$\text{MPI} = H \times A \quad (\text{Headcount Ratio } H \times \text{Intensity } A) \text{[cite: 1, 3]}$$

ONE-PAGE NOTE 5: Human Development Framework & Dynamics

1. Human Development Index (HDI)

  • Formula: Geometric mean of 3 dimension indices: $$\text{HDI} = \sqrt[3]{\text{Health} \times \text{Education} \times \text{Income}} \text{[cite: 1, 3]}$$
  • India (HDR 2022): HDI = 0.644 (Rank 134/193)[cite: 1, 3]. GII = 0.437 (Rank 108/166)[cite: 1, 3].

2. Poverty Alleviation Trajectory

  • Extreme Poverty ($2.15 Line): Dropped from 16.22% (2011–12) to 2.35% (2022–23)[cite: 1, 3].
  • LMIC Line ($3.65 Line): Stands at 28.1% (2022–23)[cite: 1, 3].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 6: Inequality Concepts, Theories & Metrics

1. Theories of Inequality

  • Marxist Theory: Capital returns exceed labor returns ($r > g$), causing exponential wealth concentration[cite: 1, 3].
  • Modern Economic Theory: Short-term inequality stems from entry barriers, firm survival disparities, and weak Rule of Law[cite: 1, 3].

2. Quantitative Metrics

  • Gini Coefficient: $\text{Gini} = \frac{a}{a+b}$ derived from Lorenz Curve[cite: 1, 3]. India's consumption Gini improved from 28.8 (2011–12) to 25.5 (2022–23)[cite: 1, 3].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 7: Unemployment Basics & Measurement

1. Key Formulas

  • Dependency Ratio: $\frac{\text{Dependents (<15 + >60)}}{\text{Working Age Population (15-59)}} \text{[cite: 2]}$
  • Unemployment Rate (UR): $\frac{\text{Unemployed}}{\text{Labour Force}} \times 100 \text{[cite: 2]}$

2. PLFS Survey Approaches

  • Usual Status (PS+SS): Headline measure combining Principal Status (>182 days) & Subsidiary Status (>30 days)[cite: 2].
  • Current Weekly Status (CWS): Worked at least 1 hour on any day in the last 7 days[cite: 2].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 8: Types of Unemployment & Growth

1. Types of Unemployment

  • Structural UE: Skill mismatch due to structural economic changes[cite: 2].
  • Disguised UE: Surplus labor with zero marginal productivity ($MP_L = 0$)[cite: 2].

2. Employment Elasticity & Employability

  • Employment Elasticity: $\frac{\% \text{ Change in Employment}}{\% \text{ Change in GDP Growth}} \text{[cite: 2]}$. Dropped to ~0.18–0.20 in India[cite: 2].
  • Employability Gap: Economic Survey 2023-24 highlights only 51% of graduates are employable[cite: 2].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 9: Skilling Architecture in India

1. Key Institutions

  • MSDE (2014): Nodal Ministry for Skill Development[cite: 2].
  • NCVET (2018): Overarching regulator formed by merging NSDA & NCVT[cite: 2].

2. Schemes

  • PMKVY: Short Term Training (STT) and Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) aligned with NSQF Levels 1-8[cite: 2].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 10: Formalisation & Voluntary Dwarfism

1. Voluntary Dwarfism

  • Stringent labour laws cause small firms to deliberately stay small or use contractual labor[cite: 2].
  • ASI FY23 Data: 41% of workers in formal manufacturing are contract workers[cite: 2].

2. Wage Hierarchy

Floor Wage ──> Minimum Wage ──> Fair Wage ──> Living Wage[cite: 2]
