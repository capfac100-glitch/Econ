ONE-PAGE NOTE 1: Poverty Fundamentals & Measurement Frameworks

1. Multi-Dimensional Definition of Poverty

  • Overarching Concept: Poverty is defined as the deprivation of multiple dimensions of well-being, including health, education, income, clean environment, social relations, security, and political voice..
  • World Bank Definition: Pronounced deprivation in well-being, comprising:
    1. Inability to acquire basic goods/services necessary for survival with dignity..
    2. Low levels of health and education..
    3. Poor access to clean water and sanitation..
    4. Inadequate physical security..
    5. Lack of voice..
    6. Insufficient capacity and opportunity to improve one's life..

2. Selection Criteria for Measurement Standards

Dimension Choice (Subjective) ──> Criteria Standard (Subjective) ──> Quantifiable Application (Objective).
  • Evaluating Criteria Options:
    • Wealth: Volatile and prone to incorrect reporting..
    • Income: Suffers from severe under-reporting and volatility..
    • Consumption Expenditure (Chosen Metric): Stable over time and yields significantly better reporting accuracy..

3. Poverty Line Basket (PLB) & Measures of Poverty

  • Poverty Line Basket (PLB): Set on the basis of expert opinion defining basic essential goods and services (food, housing, clothing, transport, education, internet).. Households affording this basket are Non-Poor..
  • Headcount Ratio (HCR): Counts how many are poor (number/percentage below Poverty Line)..
  • Depth of Poverty (Poverty Gap Index): Measures how poor the poor are (average distance from Poverty Line)..
  • Squared Poverty Gap Index (Severity of Poverty): Squares individual poverty gaps: $$\text{Squared Poverty Gap} = \frac{(GAP_1)^2 + (GAP_2)^2 + \dots + (GAP_n)^2}{N} \text{.}$$ Distributionally sensitive metric denoting the extra effort required by a person at the absolute bottom to escape poverty..
Topper's Note: While HCR measures absolute incidence, the Squared Poverty Gap Index is crucial for policy as it measures inequality among the poor..

ONE-PAGE NOTE 2: Poverty Estimation Committees in India

1. Dual Purpose Framework of Measurement

  • National Progress Tracking (Macro): Uses sampling to estimate overall national poverty levels over time..
  • Beneficiary Identification (Micro): Uses targeted scheme-specific criteria (e.g., SECC) to eliminate inclusion/exclusion errors..

2. Pre-Independence Estimations

  • Dadabhai Naoroji: Earliest poverty line based on Jail Cost of Living..
  • National Planning Committee (1938) & Bombay Plan (1944): Structural planning for basic living standards..

ONE-PAGE NOTE 3: SECC-2011 & Beneficiary Identification

1. Population Census vs SECC-2011

  • Population Census: Strictly confidential data used for macro demographic analysis..
  • SECC-2011: Open administrative dataset displayed publicly by Gram Sabhas to verify scheme eligibility..
  • Nodal Administrative Bodies: Rural SECC (Ministry of Rural Development); Urban SECC (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs); Caste Component (Ministry of Home Affairs - RGI)..

ONE-PAGE NOTE 5: Human Development Framework & Indian Dynamics

1. Human Development Index (HDI) Paradigm

  • 4 Pillars: Equity, Sustainability, Productivity, Empowerment..
  • Calculation: Geometric mean of 3 dimension indices: $$\text{HDI} = \sqrt[3]{\text{Health} \times \text{Education} \times \text{Standard of Living}} \text{.}$$ Health (Life Expectancy), Education (Mean & Expected Schooling), Income (GNI per capita PPP)..
  • India HDR 2022 Statistics:
    • HDI Value: 0.644 (Rank 134/193 - Medium HDI)..
    • IHDI: 0.444 (31.1% loss due to inequality)..
    • GDI: \(\frac{\text{Female HDI}}{\text{Male HDI}} = \frac{0.582}{0.684} = 0.862\)..
    • GII: 0.437 (Rank 108/166)..

2. India's Poverty Reduction Dynamics

  • Tendulkar Poverty Line Trajectory:

    1950 (56%) → 1993–94 (45%) → 2004–05 (37.2%) → 2009–10 (29.8%) → 2011–12 (21.9%) → 2022–23: 0.9%..

  • World Bank Equity Brief Updates (2022–23):
    • $2.15 Line (Extreme Poverty): 16.22% (2011–12) → 2.35% (~33.6M people)..
    • $3.00 Line (2021 PPP): 27.12% (2011–12) → 5.25% (~75.2M people)..
    • Rural Extreme Poverty ($2.15): 18.4% (FY12) → 2.8% (FY21) → 1.1% (FY23)..
    • Urban Extreme Poverty ($2.15): 10.7% (FY12) → 1.1% (FY23)..
    • LMIC $3.65 Line: 53% → 28.1% (Rural: 28.1%; Urban: 17.2%)..
    • Key Drivers: 5 States (UP, MP, WB, Bihar, Maharashtra) hosted 65% of poor in 2011–12 and contributed \(\frac{2}{3}\text{rd}\) of overall poverty reduction..

ONE-PAGE NOTE 6: Inequality Concepts, Theories & Metrics

1. Causes & Theories of Inequality

  • Drivers: Natural factors, political access, and the Resource Curse (resource-rich areas failing to develop due to misgovernance and social fractures)..
  • Marxist Theory (Karl Marx - Das Kapital): Capital returns exceed labor returns (\(r > g\)), causing profits to grow exponentially while wages stagnate..
  • Modern Economic Theory: Wages and profits grow at comparable rates long-term; short-term inequality stems from entry barriers, firm survival disparities, and weak Rule of Law..

2. Quantitative Measurement of Inequality

  • Lorenz Curve: Plot of cumulative percentage of national income against cumulative percentage of population..
  • Gini Coefficient: Ratio derived from Lorenz Curve: $$\text{Gini Coefficient} = \frac{a}{a + b}$$ \(\text{Gini} = 0\) (Perfect Equality); \(\text{Gini} = 1\) (Perfect Inequality)..
  • India's Consumption Gini Index: Improved from 28.8 (2011–12) to 25.5 (2022–23) based on HCES data..

3. Growth Models Comparison

Trickle-Down Model: Focus High Growth ──> Collect Tax ──> Redistribution to Passive Recipients.
Inclusive Growth Model: Capacity/Skill Building ──> Jobs ──> Active Economic Contributors.

ONE-PAGE NOTE 7: Unemployment Fundamentals & Measurement Metrics

1. Core Concepts & Demographic Framework

  • Unemployment Definition: People capable of working and actively seeking work but unable to find work[cite: 2].
  • Working Age Population: Age-based (ILO benchmark: 15–59 or 18–59 years)[cite: 2]. $$\text{Dependency Ratio} = \frac{\text{Dependents (<15 + >60)}}{\text{Working Age Population (15–59)}} \text{[cite: 2]}$$
  • Labour Force & Work Force Metrics:
    • Labour Force (LF): Employed + Unemployed[cite: 2].
    • LFPR: \(\frac{\text{Labour Force}}{\text{Total Population}}\)[cite: 2] | WPR: \(\frac{\text{Workers}}{\text{Total Population}} \times 1000\)[cite: 2].
    • Unemployment Rate (UR): \(\frac{\text{No. of Unemployed}}{\text{Labour Force}}\)[cite: 2].

2. Demographic Dividend

  • India's median age is 29 years, offering a window of opportunity up to 2055–2060[cite: 2]. High working-age population + Skilling = Demographic Dividend; without skilling = Demographic Disaster[cite: 2].

3. PLFS Measurement Methodologies

  • Usual Status (PS + SS): Principal Status (>182 days) + Subsidiary Status (>30 days). Headline measure[cite: 2].
  • Current Weekly Status (CWS): Engaged in work for at least 1 hour on any single day in preceding 7 days[cite: 2].
  • Current Daily Status (CDS): Daily intensity measure (4+ hrs = 1, 1–4 hrs = 0.5, < 1 hr = 0). Used by CMIE[cite: 2].
Measurement Rigor: Unemployment Rate (CDS) > UR (CWS) > UR (Usual Status)[cite: 2]

  • Employability Gap: Economic Survey 2023-24 highlights that only 51% of graduates are employable[cite: 2].

  • Agriculture: GDP share ~15%; Employment share ~43.4–43.9%. Low elasticity[cite: 2].

  • Services: GDP share ~59%; Employment share ~30%. Low elasticity (productivity-driven)[cite: 2].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 10: Formalisation, Dwarfism & Labour Law Deregulation

1. Dualism in the Labour Market

  • Formal vs Informal Employment: Formal employment features defined contracts, statutory rights, and social security (EPF, ESI)[cite: 2].
  • NCEUS Informality Threshold: Firms employing < 10 workers (without electricity) or < 20 workers (with electricity)[cite: 2].

2. Perverse Incentives & "Voluntary Dwarfism"

  • Rigid regulatory compliance causes small firms to stay small or turn to contractual labor[cite: 2].
  • ASI FY23 Data: 41% of workers in formal manufacturing are contract workers[cite: 2]. Small firms (< 100 workers) account for 80% of formal manufacturing firms but only 14% of employment[cite: 2].

3. Wage Hierarchy

Floor Wage ──> Minimum Wage (1,915 scheduled employments) ──> Fair Wage ──> Living Wage[cite: 2]

2. Gig & Platform Economy

  • Platform Worker: Work accessed via online aggregators/apps[cite: 2].

3. Card & Krueger Natural Experiment (Nobel Prize 2021)

  • Raising minimum wage in New Jersey ($4.25 to $5.05) did not reduce employment; demonstrated a "lighthouse effect" boosting aggregate demand[cite: 2].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 12: Consolidation into the 4 Labour Codes

Amalgamation of 29 Central Labour Laws into 4 Codes

  • Code on Wages, 2019: Consolidates 4 laws; introduces statutory National Floor Minimum Wage binding on states[cite: 2].
  • Code on Social Security, 2020: Consolidates 9 laws; sets social security funds for gig/platform workers funded by 1–2% aggregator turnover contribution[cite: 2].
  • Industrial Relations Code, 2020: Consolidates 3 laws; single negotiating union requires \(\ge 51\%\) support; raises Standing Orders threshold to 300 workers[cite: 2].
  • OSHWC Code, 2020: Consolidates 13 laws; applies to factories with 20+ workers (with power) / 40+ (without power); max 8-hr workday; portable benefits & journey allowance for inter-state migrants (wages up to ₹18,000/mo); legal right for women to work night shifts with safety provisions[cite: 2].
