ONE-PAGE NOTE 1: Poverty Fundamentals & Measurement Frameworks

1. Multi-Dimensional Definition of Poverty

  • Overarching Concept: Poverty is defined as the deprivation of multiple dimensions of well-being, including health, education, income, clean environment, social relations, security, and political voice[cite: 1].
  • World Bank Definition: Pronounced deprivation in well-being, comprising:
    1. Inability to acquire basic goods and services necessary for survival with dignity[cite: 1].
    2. Low levels of health and education[cite: 1].
    3. Poor access to clean water and sanitation[cite: 1].
    4. Inadequate physical security[cite: 1].
    5. Lack of voice[cite: 1].
    6. Insufficient capacity and opportunity to improve one's life[cite: 1].

2. Selection Criteria for Measurement Standards

Dimension Choice (Subjective) ──> Criteria Standard (Subjective) ──> Quantifiable Application (Objective)[cite: 1]
  • Evaluating Criteria Options:
    • Wealth: Highly volatile and prone to incorrect reporting[cite: 1].
    • Income: Suffer from incorrect reporting and volatility[cite: 1].
    • Consumption Expenditure (Chosen Metric): Stable and yields significantly better reporting accuracy[cite: 1].

3. Poverty Line Basket (PLB) & Measures of Poverty

  • Poverty Line Basket (PLB): Set on the basis of expert opinion to define essential goods and services (food, housing, clothing, transport, education, internet) needed for basic living[cite: 1]. Households that can afford this basket are classified as Non-Poor[cite: 1].
  • Headcount Ratio (HCR): Counts how many are poor (number/percentage of households below the Poverty Line)[cite: 1].
  • Depth of Poverty (Poverty Gap Index): Measures how poor the poor are by calculating the average shortfall distance of poor households from the Poverty Line[cite: 1].
  • Squared Poverty Gap Index (Severity of Poverty): Squares individual poverty gaps: $$\text{Squared Poverty Gap} = \frac{(GAP_1)^2 + (GAP_2)^2 + \dots + (GAP_n)^2}{N}$$ This method is distributionally sensitive and measures the severity of poverty by denoting the extra effort required by a person at the absolute bottom to escape poverty[cite: 1].
Topper's Note: The Headcount Ratio measures absolute incidence, but the Squared Poverty Gap Index is crucial for policy as it measures inequality among the poor[cite: 1].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 2: Poverty Estimation Committees in India

1. Dual Purpose Framework of Measurement

  • National Progress Tracking (Macro): Uses sampling and strict criteria to estimate overall poverty levels (being classified BPL/APL here does not directly impact individuals)[cite: 1].
  • Beneficiary Identification (Micro): Uses targeted scheme-specific criteria (e.g., SECC) to eliminate inclusion and exclusion errors in welfare delivery[cite: 1].

2. Pre-Independence Estimations

  • Dadabhai Naoroji: Formulated the earliest poverty line based on the concept of Jail Cost of Living[cite: 1].
  • National Planning Committee (1938) & Bombay Plan (1944): Initiated structural planning for minimum living standards[cite: 1].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 3: SECC-2011 & Beneficiary Identification

1. Population Census vs. SECC-2011

  • Population Census: Strictly confidential data; used for macro demographic analysis[cite: 1].
  • SECC-2011: Open administrative dataset displayed publicly by Gram Sabhas to verify beneficiary eligibility for government schemes[cite: 1].
  • Nodal Administrative Bodies: Rural SECC (Ministry of Rural Development); Urban SECC (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs); Caste Component (Ministry of Home Affairs - RGI)[cite: 1].

2. Rural SECC Architecture & Data Breakdown

                                  ┌── Automatically Included (15.95 Lakh HHs / 0.89%)[cite: 1]

  • Automatic Inclusion Criteria (0.89% / 15.95 Lakh HHs): Automatically eligible for schemes if fulfilling any of 5 parameters:
    1. Households without shelter[cite: 1].
    2. Destitute, living on alms[cite: 1].
    3. Manual scavenger families[cite: 1].
    4. Primitive Tribal Groups (PVTGs)[cite: 1].
    5. Legally released bonded labour[cite: 1].
  • Automatic Exclusion Criteria (39.35% / 7.07 Crore HHs): Excluded if fulfilling any of 14 parameters (e.g., motorized vehicles, mechanized agri equipment, KCC limit > ₹50,000, govt employee, registered non-agri enterprise, earning > ₹10,000/month, paying income/professional tax, 3+ pucca rooms, refrigerator, landline, landholding limits)[cite: 1].
  • Seven Deprivation Parameters (D1 to D7):
    • D1 (13.28% / 2.38 Cr): Households with one or less room, kuccha walls and kuccha roof[cite: 1].
    • D2 (3.64% / 65.33 Lakh): No adult member in household between age 16 and 59[cite: 1].
    • D3 (23.52% / 4.22 Cr): Households with no literate adult above age 25 years[cite: 1].
    • D4 (0.40% / 7.20 Lakh): Households with differently abled member and no other able-bodied adult[cite: 1].
    • D5 (21.56% / 3.87 Cr): SC/ST Households[cite: 1].
    • D6 (3.86% / 69.43 Lakh): Female headed household with no adult male member between 16 and 59[cite: 1].
    • D7 (30.04% / 5.40 Cr): Landless households deriving major part of income from manual casual labour[cite: 1].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 4: International Poverty Lines, SDGs & MPI

1. World Bank Income Tiers & International Poverty Lines

  • Country Income Classification (July 1, 2025 update for FY26 - GNI Per Capita):
    • Low Income (LIC): \(\le \$1,135\)[cite: 1].
    • Lower-Middle Income (LMIC): \(\$1,136 - \$4,495\)[cite: 1].
    • Upper-Middle Income (UMIC): \(\$4,496 - \$13,935\)[cite: 1].

  • International Poverty Lines (PPPDCE - Purchasing Power Parity):
    • 2017 PPP Lines (2022 Data): Extreme Poverty (LIC) = $2.15; LMIC = $3.65; UMIC = $6.85[cite: 1].
    • 2021 PPP Lines (2025 Update): Extreme Poverty (LIC) = $3.00; LMIC = $4.20; UMIC = $8.30[cite: 1].

2. MDGs to SDGs Evolution

  • MDGs (2000–2015): 8 goals approved by UNGA[cite: 1]. MDG 1 targeted eradicating extreme poverty and hunger[cite: 1].
  • SDGs (2015–2030): Agreed at Rio+20 (2012) and adopted at UN Summit 2015 (New York)[cite: 1]. Comprises 17 Goals and 169 Targets[cite: 1].
    • SDG Goal 1: No Poverty — completely eliminate extreme poverty by 2030[cite: 1].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 5: Human Development Framework & Indian Dynamics

1. Human Development Index (HDI) Paradigm

  • 4 Pillars: Equity, Sustainability, Productivity, Empowerment[cite: 1].
  • Approaches: Income, Welfare, Basic Needs (ILO: Health, Education, Food, Water supply, Sanitation, Housing), Capability Approach (Amartya Sen)[cite: 1].
  • HDI Calculation (UNDP HDR): Geometric mean of 3 dimension indices: $$\text{HDI} = \sqrt[3]{H \times E \times S}$$ Health (Life Expectancy at Birth), Education (Mean & Expected Years of Schooling), Standard of Living (GNI per capita PPP)[cite: 1].
  • India HDR 2022 Statistics:
    • HDI Value: 0.644 (Rank 134/193 - Medium HDI)[cite: 1].
    • IHDI (Inequality-adjusted HDI): 0.444 (31.1% loss due to inequality)[cite: 1].
    • GDI (Gender Development Index): \(\frac{\text{Female HDI}}{\text{Male HDI}} = \frac{0.582}{0.684} = 0.862\) (Group 5)[cite: 1].
    • GII (Gender Inequality Index): 0.437 (Rank 108/166)[cite: 1]. Dimensions: Health (MMR, Adolescent birth rate), Empowerment (Parliamentary seats, Secondary education), Labour market participation[cite: 1].

2. India's Poverty Reduction Dynamics

  • Tendulkar Poverty Line Trajectory:

    1950 (56%) → 1993–94 (45%) → 2004–05 (37.2%) → 2009–10 (29.8%) → 2011–12 (21.9%) → 2022–23: 0.9%[cite: 1].

  • World Bank Poverty & Equity Brief (India Updates):
    • $2.15 Line (Extreme Poverty): 16.22% (2011–12) → 2.35% (~33.6M people in 2022–23)[cite: 1].
    • $3.00 Line (2021 PPP): 27.12% (2011–12) → 5.25% (~75.2M people in 2022–23)[cite: 1].
    • Rural Extreme Poverty ($2.15): 18.4% (FY12) → 2.8% (FY21) → 1.1% (FY23)[cite: 1].
    • Urban Extreme Poverty ($2.15): 10.7% (FY12) → 1.1% (FY23)[cite: 1].
    • LMIC Line ($3.65 Line): Declined from 53% to 28.1% in 2022–23 (Rural: 61.8% → 28.1%; Urban: 43.5% → 17.2%)[cite: 1].
    • Key Drivers: 5 States (UP, MP, Maharashtra, WB, Bihar) hosted 65% of extreme poor in 2011–12 and contributed \(\frac{2}{3}\text{rd}\) of overall poverty reduction by 2022–23[cite: 1].

ONE-PAGE NOTE 6: Inequality Concepts, Theories & Metrics

1. Causes & Theories of Inequality

  • Manifestations: Economic, Social, Political[cite: 1]. Driven by natural factors, political access, and the Resource Curse (resource-rich areas failing to develop due to misgovernance and fractured society)[cite: 1].
  • Marxist Theory (Karl Marx - Das Kapital): Society splits into Bourgeoisie (capital/land owners) and Proletariat (laborers)[cite: 1]. Rate of return on capital exceeds return on labor (\(r > g\)), causing profits to grow exponentially while wages remain stagnant[cite: 1].
  • Modern Economic Theory: Contradicts Marx over the long run; wages and profits grow at comparable rates[cite: 1]. Short-term inequality stems from failure of firm survival, entry barriers, unfair practices, and lack of Rule of Law[cite: 1].

2. Quantitative Measurement of Inequality

  • Lorenz Curve: Graphs cumulative percentage of total national income against cumulative percentage of population[cite: 1].
  • Gini Coefficient: Derived from the Lorenz Curve: $$\text{Gini Coefficient} = \frac{a}{a + b}$$ Where \(a\) is the area between Line of Absolute Equality and Lorenz Curve, and \(a+b\) is the total area under the Line of Equality[cite: 1].
    • \(\text{Gini} = 0\): Perfect Equality[cite: 1].
    • \(\text{Gini} = 1\): Perfect Inequality[cite: 1].
  • India's Consumption Gini Index: Improved from 28.8 (2011–12) to 25.5 (2022–23) based on HCES data, placing India among the world's most equal societies in consumption[cite: 1].

3. Growth Models Comparison

Trickle-Down Model: Focus High Growth ──> Collect Tax Revenue ──> Redistribution to Passive Recipients[cite: 1]
Inclusive Growth Model: Skill & Capacity Building ──> Jobs & Opportunities ──> Active Economic Contributors[cite: 1]
